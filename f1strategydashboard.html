The headline surprise

The undercut is F1's most talked-about strategy call — but across the full unfiltered dataset, it shows a small net loss on average. The overcut (staying out longer) consistently outperformed it, in every season from 2011 to 2017.

Why this isn't the full story: a raw average across every stop treats a driver pitting from P1 the same as one pitting from P18 — very different amounts of "room" to gain or lose. The breakdown below, by constructor, shows why context matters more than the headline number.

Undercut value by constructor

Limited to constructors with at least 15 undercut attempts. Marussia and Caterham top the list — both backmarker teams that spent most races lapped, pitting into clean air with little midfield traffic to defend against, rather than showing superior strategy calling. Mercedes' large negative average reflects the opposite effect: starting near the front leaves far more room to lose positions than gain them.

Undercut value by circuit

Limited to circuits with at least 10 undercut attempts. Circuits where overtaking is genuinely difficult on track — Albert Park, Monza, Barcelona — show the biggest undercut payoff, since track position won in the pits is harder to lose back on lap time alone.

Methodology

Each driver's first pit stop per race (2011–2017 seasons only, the earliest years with complete pit stop timing in this dataset) was classified as an undercut, overcut, or inline call by comparing its lap number to that race's median first-stop lap across the field (±2 laps as the threshold). Positions gained/lost is grid position minus classified finishing position. This is a simplification of "expected strategy" — it doesn't account for safety car periods, which can distort pit windows independent of a team's strategic skill.
